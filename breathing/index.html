About this breathing ↓

Background

About 4-7-8 breathing

4-7-8 breathing is also called the Relaxing Breath. You breathe in through your nose for 4 counts, hold your breath for 7 counts and breathe out through your mouth for 8 counts. So the out-breath lasts twice as long as the in-breath.1,2

Where does it come from?

The American physician Andrew Weil, known for integrative medicine, made the exercise popular. It is inspired by pranayama, the breathing practices of yoga.1,3

How to do it

  1. Sit up straight. Before sleep you can also simply lie in bed.1
  2. Rest the tip of your tongue on the ridge just behind your upper front teeth and keep it there for the whole exercise.
  3. Breathe out completely through your mouth, with a soft whooshing sound.
  4. Close your mouth and breathe in quietly through your nose while counting to 4 in your head.
  5. Hold your breath, relaxed, and count to 7.
  6. Breathe out audibly through your mouth and count to 8. That is one round.
  7. Repeat until you have done four rounds. Weil advises no more than four rounds in a row during the first month; later you can go up to eight. Practise at least twice a day.2

What matters is the 4 : 7 : 8 ratio, not exact seconds. If holding is hard, simply count a little faster. With the pace setting above you can count slower or quicker; the ratio stays the same.2

What is it for?

The exercise is meant to help you settle down. It may help with tension and worrying, and with falling asleep more easily. The slow breathing may help your nervous system relax, and the counting gives your mind something other than your worries to focus on.1,2

The scientific evidence is still limited. In a small study of 43 healthy young adults, heart rate and systolic blood pressure dropped right after a few sets of 4-7-8 breathing.4 Whether it really makes you fall asleep faster has not been well studied.3 If you have been sleeping badly for weeks, talk to your doctor.

Note. At first you may feel a little lightheaded.1,2 If you get dizzy, short of breath or feel unwell, stop and breathe normally.
