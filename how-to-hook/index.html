How to Hook: a step-by-step workshop for writing your own first line

The 20 hooks give you templates. This page shows how to turn your own idea into a first line that earns the "Show more" click.

The 7 steps

1Know your one point and audience

Write down, in one sentence, what the post says and who it is for. If you can't, the hook won't fix it. One post, one point.

2Pick the hook type

Match the type to what the post does:

  • A result → Number, Proof or Transformation
  • An opinion → Contrarian, Prediction or Myth-Busting
  • A lesson → Mistake, List or How-To
  • A story → Story, Open-Loop or Challenge
  • A specific group → Direct Callout or Relatable

3Draft 5–10 versions

Don't stop at the first line. Write it with two or three different hook types and several wordings. The first draft is rarely the best one.

4Cut to the bone

The first line should fit before "Show more". Remove warm-ups ("So...", "I've been thinking"), filler words and anything the reader doesn't need yet.

5Add specificity

Swap vague words for numbers, names and stakes. "Grow faster" becomes "go from 200 to 5,000 followers". Specific lines are easier to believe and picture.

6Check it delivers on the promise

Read the hook, then the post. Does the post pay it off fully? If not, change the hook or improve the post. Never publish a promise you don't keep.

7Test and vary

Rotate hook types across posts and notice which ones get reads and replies from your audience. Keep what works, drop what doesn't.

Worked example

The post: what I learned from sharing drafts with three friends before publishing.

Start
So I've been thinking a lot lately about writing and feedback and stuff.
1. Point and audience
Point: getting feedback on drafts makes posts better. Audience: people who write on X but rarely get replies.
2. Hook type
It's a small experiment → Challenge Hook, or a lesson → Mistake Hook.
3. Drafts
  • I shared every draft with friends for a month. Here's what happened.
  • My biggest mistake was posting without feedback.
  • Nobody talks about this, but your friends are your best editors.
  • If you never get replies, read this.
  • Feedback changed my writing.
4. Cut
I shared every draft with friends for a month. Here's what happened.
5. Specificity
I sent every draft to 3 friends for 30 days. Here's what happened.
6. Promise check
The post must say what actually changed, good and bad. It does, so the hook stays.
7. Test
Next time, try the Mistake version for a similar post and compare.
Result
I sent every draft to 3 friends for 30 days. Here's what happened.

Printable checklist

  • I can say the point of this post in one sentence.
  • I know who it is for.
  • The hook type fits what the post does.
  • I wrote at least 5 versions.
  • The first line fits before "Show more".
  • No warm-up or filler words.
  • It has a number, name or clear stakes.
  • The post fully delivers on the hook.
  • I haven't used this exact template in my last few posts.
