How the X algorithm works

Straight from X's open-source code on GitHub, in plain English.

Share on XX Algorithm guide

In one sentence: X guesses, for every post it could show you, how likely you are to like it, reply, share it, read it, or mute the author, then adds those guesses up with fixed weights and shows the highest totals first.

This page is built only from X's own published code and READMEs on GitHub. Every number links to the file it came from. Anything I could not confirm in the code is marked uncertain.

On this page: The simple version · How a feed is built · The weights · What changed over time · The 2023 system · What it means for you · Myths vs facts · Glossary · FAQ · Sources

1. The simple version

Think of the For You feed as a store that restocks a shelf just for you, every time you open the app.

  1. Gather. It grabs a pile of recent posts: some from people you follow, some from people you don't.
  2. Throw out. It removes posts you've seen, posts older than 48 hours, posts from accounts you block or mute, and posts matching your muted words.
  3. Guess. A model looks at what you recently did on X and predicts how likely you are to take each action on each post.
  4. Add up. Those predictions are multiplied by weights and summed into one score.
  5. Tidy. It spreads out repeated authors, discounts posts from strangers a bit, gives small new accounts a lift, and avoids showing near-identical posts side by side.
  6. Check. A separate safety system decides whether each post may be shown at all.

Source: xai-org/x-algorithm README (Overview, How It Works, Filtering).

2. How a feed is built (diagram)

1Request: you open For You
↓
2Query hydration: your recent engagements, follows, blocks, mutes, muted words, posts already seen
↓
3Candidate sources: Thunder (people you follow) + Phoenix retrieval and SimClusters (people you don't) + popular posts
↓
4Pre-scoring filters (duplicates, older than 48 h, your own posts, blocked/muted, seen before …)
↓
5Phoenix ranking: predicted probability of each action
↓
6Score = Σ weight × P(action), then author diversity, out-of-network discount, new-author boost
↓
7VMRanker: reorders to avoid showing very similar posts next to each other
↓
8Visibility filtering + Blending pipeline (ads, Who to Follow, prompts) → your feed

Source: README: System Architecture, Components, pipeline in phoenix_candidate_pipeline.rs, filters in home-mixer/filters/.

The parts, one line each

Home Mixer
Builds the feed: pipeline stages, scoring weights, calls to other systems.
Thunder (in-network)
Holds recent posts in memory and returns those from accounts you follow.
Phoenix retrieval (out-of-network)
Turns you and every post into vectors and returns posts closest to you.
SimClusters (out-of-network)
Groups accounts and posts by who engages with what, then uses those groups to find posts.
Popular posts
A shared list of popular recent posts, initially from the most-followed active accounts.
Phoenix ranking
The transformer that predicts each action. Training and serving code in JAX and Rust.
VMRanker
Reorders with a determinantal point process: gives up a little score for less similarity between neighbors.
Visibility filtering
Decides show, drop, or show behind a warning, using your blocks/mutes and labels from systems like Grox, agatha, user-cred-v2 and botmaker rules.

Also in that file: BidirectionalFollowReplyWeightBoost = 15.0 and BidirectionalFollowDwellWeightBoost = 0.0. The names point to an extra reply weight when you and the author follow each other; I did not trace exactly how it is applied uncertain.

Read this before quoting the numbers. The weights multiply the predicted probability that you take an action, not raw counts. X's own comment: the baseline chance of a report is "more than 1000x lower than a Like", so it gets a big weight just to matter at all. "One report cancels 468 likes" is explicitly called incorrect in the README and in param.rs.

Note: the README's "468 times" example matches a like weight of 0.5 (234 ÷ 0.5). The current file says 0.66, which gives about 355×. The example may predate a change uncertain.

6. What it means for posting and replying

These follow from the code. The size of any effect on your account isn't published.

  • Write for replies, quotes and shares, not likes. Reply, quote and DM share are 5.0, copy link 20.0, like 0.66. See Reply on X and How to Reply.
  • Earn the read. Dwell, dwell time and click dwell time are scored, and the Oct 2026 updates target time spent and reading all the way through. A strong first line matters: Hooks on X.
  • Avoid "not interested", mutes and reports. Bait that annoys people can cost more than it earns. → Negative signals
  • Freshness matters. Posts older than 48 hours are filtered out of For You. → Timing
  • Media is scored lightly. Photo expand 0.05, video open 0.02. → Media and video
  • Shares are the top positive weights. Copy link 20, DM 5, share 2. → Shares on X · Share Hooks
  • Follows and mutual follows count. Follow author 4.0, plus a mutual-follow reply boost. → Growing followers
  • Replies from strangers don't travel out-of-network. OONRetweetReplyFilter removes reposts and replies from accounts the viewer doesn't follow. Your reply mainly reaches your followers and the people in that thread.

7. Myths vs facts

MythWhat the code says
"One report cancels 468 likes."False, per X's own README and code comments: weights scale predicted probabilities, not counts.
"A reply is worth 75 likes."That came from 2023 (reply engaged by author 75 vs like 0.5). It's not in the 2026 param.rs, and it was never a count conversion.
"Grok picks your feed."Overstated. Phoenix is a transformer recommender; an earlier sample was ported from Grok-1. Grox uses LLM prompts for labeling, and those prompts aren't published uncertain.
"Links are banned or penalized."Open link has a positive weight (0.2). No link penalty appears in param.rs. Other effects elsewhere aren't ruled out uncertain.
"Paying for Premium is a ranking weight."No subscription weight appears in param.rs. IneligibleSubscriptionFilter only hides subscriber-only posts. Other boosts elsewhere aren't ruled out uncertain.
"Old posts can go viral in For You days later."AgeFilter removes posts older than 48 hours from For You.
"Everyone sees the same ranking."No. Predictions are personal: they're driven by each viewer's own action history.

8. Glossary

In-network / out-of-network (OON)
Posts from accounts you follow / accounts you don't.
Candidate
A post that might be shown, before ranking.
Retrieval
Finding candidates, e.g. nearest vectors in Phoenix's two-tower model.
Ranker
The model that scores candidates.
Weight
A multiplier on a predicted probability.
Dwell
Time spent on a post.
Embedding
A list of numbers that represents a user or post so similar ones sit close together.
SimClusters
Communities of accounts and posts found from engagement.
TwHIN
2023 graph embeddings for users and posts.
RealGraph
2023 model of how likely two users are to interact.
Light / heavy ranker
2023 fast pre-ranker / full neural ranker.
Visibility filtering
Decides whether a post can be shown at all.
Under the Hood
X's tool that shows the visibility labels on your own account and posts.

9. FAQ

Is this really the production code?
The README says it's the core code for For You. Some files are withheld (Grox prompts, some botmaker rules). Production values may be changed through configuration, so live values can differ uncertain.
Are the weights fixed?
They're defaults declared as params, and experiments can override them ("Experiments and Configuration" in the README).
Does this cover Following, Search or notifications?
The new repo covers For You. The 2023 repo also covered Recommended Notifications.
Can I check whether my account is limited?
Yes. The README describes Under the Hood reports for your account and posts.
How much does a reply boost my reach?
Not published. The weight applies to a viewer's predicted chance of replying, not to your counts.
