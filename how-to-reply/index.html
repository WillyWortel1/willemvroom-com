How to Reply

Write replies on X that read like posts worth reading on their own, and turn them into follows.

Open the Reply AppShare on X

Most replies on X are comments: “Great post!”, “100% agree”, a fire emoji. They add nothing, so nobody follows. This workshop shows you how to turn every reply into a miniature piece of content. It builds on Does it pay to be a reply guy?, which explains what X’s open-source algorithm says about replies.

The 7 steps

  1. Pick the right conversation. A post in your niche where you actually know something. Relevance beats reach.
  2. Read the whole post. And the top replies. Don’t repeat what someone already said.
  3. Choose one angle. An example, a tactic, a number, a counterargument, a question, your experience. One, not three.
  4. Lead with the point. No warm-up, no “Great post, but”. Your first words carry the value.
  5. Add one concrete detail. A number, a named tool, a step, a real result. This is what makes it worth reading.
  6. Give the author a reason to reply. A sharp question or a point they’ll want to answer. X says the author replying to you is one of the signals for reply placement.
  7. Check and post. 60–280 characters, no links, no self-promo, not a copy of your last reply. Then come back and respond when people engage.

Worked examples across niches

Same idea every time: take the post one step further with something only you can add.

“Launched on Product Hunt, got 300 upvotes and 4 paying users.”

Post

“You don’t need more motivation, you need a routine.”

“What made it stick for me: training at the same time every day, even for 15 minutes on bad days. After 6 weeks I stopped deciding and just went.”

Templates for every angle

Fill in the brackets with your own facts. Never invent numbers. If you don’t have one, pick a different angle.

1Add an example

Make an abstract point concrete.

Example of this: [specific situation]. [What happened] → [result].

A real one I saw: [who/what], [what they did], [outcome].

Example

Example of this: I cut my posting from 6 a day to 2 and added 10 thoughtful replies. Profile visits went up 40% in 3 weeks because people actually read the replies.

2Share a tactic

Give one move people can use today.

One underrated tactic: [action]. Then [next step]. [Why it works].

If you want to try this: 1) [step] 2) [step] 3) [step].

Example

One underrated prompting tactic: paste 3 of your best posts as examples before asking for a draft. Matches your voice far better than any style description.

3Add a number

Back the point with a data point.

Data point: [number] [metric] after [change], over [time].

We tested this: [A] got [x], [B] got [y]. Difference: [z].

Example

Data point: moving my link from the first post to the first reply took profile visits from 0.8% to 2.1% across 14k impressions.

4Respectful counterargument

Disagree with reasons, not attitude.

Mostly agree, but [exception]: [reason]. In that case [alternative].

Counterpoint: [claim] works for [group], not for [other group], because [reason].

Example

Mostly agree, but for new accounts I’d skip this: with no followers yet, 10 deep replies beat 50 quick posts. Depth first, volume later.

5Ask a sharp question

One specific question the author wants to answer.

Curious: did [specific variable] change, or was it only [factor]?

How do you decide between [option A] and [option B] when [situation]?

Example

Curious: did you change the prompt too, or was the better output only from switching models?

6Personal experience

What happened when you did it.

Did this last [time period]. [What I did], [result]. Biggest surprise: [insight].

I tried the opposite: [what]. Result: [result]. So [lesson].

Example

Tried this on my Tesla: Autopilot on the same commute for 2 weeks, then 2 weeks without. Arrived noticeably less tired with it, but range dropped about 3% in winter.

7Common mistake

Warn about the pitfall people hit next.

The mistake I see after this: [mistake]. Fix: [fix].

Careful with [step]: if you [wrong way], you get [bad result].

Example

The mistake I see after this: people paste the AI draft straight into X. Add one real detail of your own first, or it reads like every other reply.

8Step-by-step

Turn the idea into a mini how-to.

How I’d do this in [time]: 1) [step] 2) [step] 3) [step].

Quick version: [step], then [step], then check [metric] after [time].

Example

How I’d do this in an hour: 1) pick 10 accounts in your niche 2) turn on notifications for them 3) reply within 15 minutes with one real example.

9Build on it (yes, and)

Extend the author’s point one step further.

Yes, and [next level]: [detail].

Adding to this: it gets even better when you [extra step], because [reason].

Example

Yes, and the same works for old tweets: find your top 10 by bookmarks, rewrite the hook, and repost. Two of mine did better the second time.

10Tool or workflow

Share how you actually do it.

My workflow for this: [tool] for [job], then [tool] for [job]. Takes [time].

Small trick in [tool]: [setting/feature] does [result].

Example

My workflow for this: Grok to spot what’s trending in my niche, then a note with 10 target accounts to reply to. Takes 20 minutes a day.

11Add nuance (it depends)

Name when it does and doesn’t apply.

Depends on [factor]. If [case A], [advice A]. If [case B], [advice B].

True for [context]. In [other context] I’d do [alternative] instead.

Example

Depends on account size. Under 1k followers: reply more, you need reach. Over 10k: post more original threads first.

12Sum up the key insight

Distill the post into one sharp line plus why.

The key line here: [insight]. Most people miss it because [reason].

Short version for anyone skimming: [insight], not [common belief].

Example

Short version for anyone skimming: X rewards the best reply in a thread, not the most replies.

Big accounts vs small accounts

Big accountsSmall and mid accounts
CompetitionHundreds of replies in minutesA handful, you can be the best one
Author repliesRareCommon, and that helps placement
Who readsBroad audience, many not in your nicheExactly the people you want
Best angleA sharp number or counterpoint, very earlyQuestions, experience, building on their point

Don’t chase the biggest accounts. Chase the most relevant conversations. A good mix: 70% accounts your size or a bit bigger, 30% big accounts where you have something genuinely useful.

Timing: reply early

  • The first useful replies get seen most. Turn on notifications for your 10–20 target accounts.
  • Aim for the first 15–60 minutes after a post goes up.
  • Late is fine when you add something new. Late and generic is pointless.
  • Come back an hour later and reply to people who replied to you. Those conversations are where follows happen.

Disagreeing respectfully

Counterarguments get attention, good and bad. Keep them good:

  1. Agree with what’s true first. “Mostly agree…”
  2. Name the specific exception. “…but for new accounts…”
  3. Give the reason or data. “…with no followers yet, 10 deep replies beat 50 quick posts.”
  4. Offer the alternative. “Depth first, volume later.”
Before“Wrong. This is terrible advice.”
After“Mostly agree, but for new accounts I’d skip this: with no followers yet, 10 deep replies beat 50 quick posts. Depth first, volume later.”

No sarcasm, no quote-dunking, no “actually”. Negative feedback such as mutes, blocks and reports is a signal you never want to earn.

Turning replies into follows

A great reply gets someone to tap your name. Then your profile has three seconds:

  • Name and photo: clear and recognizable, the same everywhere.
  • Bio: who you help and with what. “AI, Tesla and growing on X. Daily tests with real numbers.” beats “Dad. Coffee. Thoughts.”
  • Pinned post: your best post or thread on the exact topic you reply about. It’s the proof.
  • Recent posts: the 30-day experiment includes 1 useful original post a day, so there’s always something fresh to read.

The daily routine

  • Publish 1 useful original post
  • Check your 10–20 target accounts for new posts
  • Leave 5–10 thoughtful replies in your niche
  • Add examples, original insights or respectful counterarguments
  • Respond when authors and readers engage
  • Track profile visits, relevant followers and conversations

About 30–45 minutes a day. Do it for 30 days before you judge it. The Reply App has a local tracker with a streak and a chart.

Checklist before you post

  • Not generic (no “Great post!”, “Agree”, “100%”)
  • 60–280 characters
  • Adds something specific
  • One idea
  • No links, no self-promo
  • Not a copy of your last replies
  • Gives the author a reason to reply
  • Would it work as a post on its own?

FAQ

How long should a reply be?
Long enough to add one real thing. Under about 60 characters rarely does. One to three sentences is the sweet spot, and it must fit in 280.
Should I reply to the biggest accounts?
Not first. Big accounts bury you in hundreds of replies. Pick 10–20 relevant accounts in your niche where you can be one of the first useful voices, and add a few bigger ones you genuinely have something for.
Does replying boost my account by +5?
No. In X’s published For You scoring a reply has a weight of +5.0 versus +0.66 for a like, but those weights multiply predicted engagement probabilities. They aren’t points awarded per action, and replying doesn’t automatically give your account a +5 boost.
Can bad replies hurt?
The published weights include Not interested −52.272, Mute −58.8, Block −31.2 and Report −234. They’re prediction weights, not per-action penalties, but spraying irrelevant, repetitive replies is a terrible growth strategy.
Is it okay to disagree?
Yes, respectful counterarguments are one of the best reply types. Agree with what’s true, name the exception, give your reason and offer an alternative. Attack the idea, never the person.
Can I put a link in a reply?
Avoid it. Links in replies look like spam and pull people off the post. Let the reply do the work and keep the link on your profile or pinned post.
Can I use AI to write replies?
Use it to draft and sharpen, not to spray. Give it the post, your angle and your own facts, then edit until it sounds like you. The Reply App has bot prompts for this.
How do I measure if it works?
Track profile visits, relevant new followers and real conversations for 30 days, not likes. The Reply App has a local 30-day tracker.
