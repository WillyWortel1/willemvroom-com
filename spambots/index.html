Losing followers overnight isn't always bad news.

Here's what spambots on X are, why your follower count sometimes drops for no clear reason, how bots hurt your reach, and what you can do about them.

Share on XX Algorithm guide

What counts as a "bot"

People use "bot" for several kinds of accounts:

  • Spambots. Automated accounts that post the same links, replies or DMs over and over.
  • Fake followers. Accounts made to inflate someone's follower count. They're often sold in bulk.
  • Reply bots. Accounts that jump into the replies of popular posts with spam, scams or generic AI text. In 2024, TechCrunch found verified bots replying with "I'm sorry, I cannot provide a response as it goes against OpenAI's use case policy". That's a clear sign there was no human behind them.
  • Crypto and porn bots. Two common types. One pushes fake giveaways and "investment" links. The other posts explicit replies that send you to a link in their bio. New York Intelligencer reported on the second type in 2024.
  • Ghost or inactive followers. These aren't always bots. They're real accounts that stopped using X. They still count as followers, but they never see or interact with your posts.

X's Platform Manipulation and Spam policy prohibits artificial amplification, coordinated fake accounts, misleading automation and spammy engagement. Most of the accounts above break it.

Why your follower count drops

When X removes accounts that break its rules, they also disappear from the follower lists of everyone they followed. You don't have to do anything for that to happen.

X has done this in public several times:

  • April 4, 2024. @Safety announced "a significant, proactive initiative to eliminate accounts that violate our Rules against platform manipulation and spam." X said it was "casting a wide net" and warned that follower counts could change. Social Media Today reported that Elon Musk lost about 43,000 followers in 24 hours, and Barack Obama and Justin Bieber about 20,000 each.
  • October 12, 2025. X's Head of Product, Nikita Bier: "This week we purged 1.7 million bots engaging in reply spam." He said DM spam was next.
  • April 9, 2026. Bier said X was "identifying and suspending 208 bots per minute."

The scale is large. According to The Guardian, X told UK MPs it suspended 800 million accounts in 2024 for breaking its platform manipulation and spam rules. That figure comes from X's own testimony, and X didn't say how the suspensions break down.

Big accounts lose the most in a purge because bots follow famous people to look real. Small accounts lose a few. If your count drops by a handful overnight while your engagement stays the same, a cleanup is the likely reason.

Sweeps aren't perfect. X itself said it was casting a wide net, and it shared an appeal form for accounts caught by mistake.

How to spot a bot

There's no official checklist, but X's own filters show what it treats as risky. You can filter notifications from accounts you don't follow that:

  • are new
  • don't follow you
  • have a default profile photo
  • haven't confirmed an email address or phone number

Other signs you can see yourself:

  • A random username full of digits, with no bio or a bio that's only a link
  • Generic replies that would fit under any post ("Great insight!", "So true 🔥")
  • The same text posted under many accounts
  • A crypto, giveaway or "check my bio" pitch
  • Hundreds of accounts followed, few followers, and almost no original posts

One sign alone proves nothing. Lots of real people have no profile photo. Look for several signs together.

How bots hurt your reach

On X, your follower count isn't what decides your reach. What people do with your posts decides it.

X has published its recommendation algorithm on GitHub (xai-org/x-algorithm). The ranking model predicts how likely someone is to take certain actions and gives each action a weight. The default weights there include:

Positive signals

  • Share via copy link: 20
  • Reply: 5
  • Like: 0.66

Negative signals

  • Report: −234
  • Mute: −58.8
  • Not interested: −52.3
  • Block: −31.2

Two things follow from this.

1. Bots dilute your engagement. A follower who never likes, replies or shares adds nothing to these signals. A follower list full of bots and ghosts looks big but adds no engagement.

2. Bot replies can make your posts less pleasant to read. If your replies fill up with porn or crypto spam, readers have reasons to mute, block or tap "not interested". Those signals weigh far more than a like. One report weighs as much as hundreds of likes. The weights describe predicted actions, not a penalty that's applied automatically. But a spam-filled thread is the kind of place people leave with a negative action.

Fake followers also skew your own data. If 20% of your followers aren't real, your engagement rate looks worse than it is, and it gets harder to tell which posts actually worked.

What to do about it

Here are the tools X gives you, from lightest to heaviest:

Mute. You stop seeing an account's posts. It isn't notified and still follows you. Use it for people you want to keep but not hear from.

Remove this follower. On x.com, open the profile, click the three dots and choose "Remove this follower." The account stops following you and isn't notified, but it can follow you again. This is web-only. A recent test found it missing from the iPhone and Android apps.

Block. The account can't follow you, reply, like or repost. Since a 2024 change, blocked accounts can still see your public posts. Use this for clear spam that keeps coming back.

Report. If an account is plainly a spambot, report it as spam. That's how X's systems learn which accounts to remove.

Turn on the quality filter. Under Notifications settings, the quality filter hides lower-quality content, such as duplicate posts or content that looks automated. It never filters accounts you follow or have recently interacted with. X turns it on by default for new accounts.

Use advanced notification filters. You can mute notifications from new accounts, accounts with a default photo, or accounts without a confirmed email or phone number.

Limit replies. Before or after posting, you can let only accounts you follow, accounts you mention, or verified accounts reply. When X added the verified-only option in October 2023, Musk said it "should help a lot with spam bots." Quotes stay open. Verified doesn't always mean human, as the 2024 reply bots showed.

Never buy followers. Buying followers is exactly what the spam policy prohibits, and paid followers are the first ones removed in a sweep.

You don't need to go through your follower list one by one. X has no bulk remove. For a small account, the quality filter, an occasional block and reporting obvious spam are enough.

Why real followers matter more than the count

A follower count is a vanity number. Reach on X comes from people who reply, share and come back. An account with 255 real people who read and respond beats one with 2,550 accounts that never show up.

So when a purge takes a few followers, it's not a loss. Your number now matches your actual audience more closely, and your engagement rate is more honest.

Focus on what the algorithm weighs most: posts worth replying to and worth sharing. Keep your replies clean so readers don't leave with a mute or a block. The count will follow.
