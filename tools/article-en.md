# Kraków shooting: timeline, locations and reactions

On Saturday, 7 November 2026, a shooting takes place in the centre of Kraków. Six people are killed. Three more bodies are later found in the suspect's home. The investigation links two places: Kraków's historic old town and Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, where the suspect lives.

Point 1 is the Main Market Square, Rynek Główny in Polish: the area of the shooting. Point 2 is the junction of Dietla and Stradomska streets, where the suspect was arrested.

It begins on Saturday afternoon at around 3 p.m., at the Main Market Square in the historic centre. A man fires at people from a car. The emergency call centre receives reports and emergency services are sent to the centre. Visitors and passers-by are on and around the square at the time.

Within minutes, municipal wardens and police officers are at the scene. An exchange of fire follows and officers give chase. Ambulances and fire crews arrive to help. Paramedics treat the victims while police try to bring the situation under control. Two police officers are injured during the operation.

Officers shoot and arrest the suspect at the junction of Dietla and Stradomska. He is taken to hospital. Police cordon off parts of the centre and begin securing evidence. Witnesses are asked to come forward and share any footage with investigators.

Regional police confirm six people killed in the shooting: four women and two men. Later that evening, three bodies are found in the suspect's home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski: two women and a man.

The investigation continues through the night. On Sunday morning, Polish prosecutors confirm that both cases are being investigated together: the six deaths in Kraków and the three in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski. According to prosecutors, the evidence gathered points to the same person. Investigators are working at both locations.

Ostrowiec lies north-east of Kraków. That is where the three bodies were found in the home.

On Sunday, the Main Market Square and parts of the route through the centre are cordoned off again for further forensic work. Meanwhile, many people come to give blood for the injured. Around midday, regional police report that the blood centre's supplies are full and ask people to postpone further visits.

Eyewitnesses describe how confusing the first moments were. Some thought it was fireworks. A waitress describes how her manager told everyone to take cover and guests crawled under the tables. A boutique owner on Grodzka Street let ten people in, locked the door and turned off the lights. On Dietla Street, a stranger pulled a passer-by around the corner. He initially thought a film was being shot.

One of the injured officers, Jakub Kulik, is also a volunteer firefighter in Bielsko-Biała. Władysław Szypuła, head of his fire brigade, says how deeply the news has affected him. His main wish is for Jakub to recover. He describes him as someone for whom helping others came first.

Kraków's Academy of Fine Arts has also responded. The suspect's sister, who was found dead with her parents in Ostrowiec, studied painting there. The academy pays tribute to her and has declared mourning until 11 November.

In Ostrowiec, neighbour Zdzisław Szostak says he knew the parents personally and celebrated New Year's Eve with them. He remembers them as cheerful, open people.

Poland is observing national mourning on 8 and 9 November. Flags fly at half-mast and major events are cancelled. In Kraków, city mourning lasts until 11 November inclusive. Trams and buses also carry flags with black ribbons. At St Mary's Basilica, on the edge of the Main Market Square, residents and visitors lay flowers and light candles for the victims.

The situation on Sunday afternoon: nine dead in two cases being investigated together. The suspect has been arrested and is in hospital.
